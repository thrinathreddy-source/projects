Producing the first batch

Everything between "the spec is decided" and "100 units exist that can legally be sold." Ordered by dependency, gated by decisions that let you stop cheaply.

Read this before anything else

₹18,000 is a volume number, not your first-batch number. It assumes production quantities you will not have on day one. At qty 100 you get no volume pricing on the display, and every one-time engineering cost — design, tooling, certification, failed PCB spins — divides across only 100 units instead of thousands.

The phases

P0
Weeks 1–4
₹1.5–2.5L

Prove the display before you believe anything else

Buy one JBD Hummingbird Mini development kit. Not two, not a batch — one. Every downstream decision, cost, and month assumes green monochrome text on a waveguide is genuinely readable for the people this is for. That assumption is unverified and it is cheap to test now and catastrophic to test late.

  • Contact JBD directly (they are the confirmed supplier behind G1 and RayNeo). Ask for the monochrome dev kit and a price at 100 / 1,000 / 5,000 units in the same email.
  • That quote is the single most important number in this entire plan — it decides whether the ₹18k target is real.
Gate — do not pass without this

Can a farm worker read a full sentence of Telugu on it, outdoors, at midday, without shading their eyes? If no: the whole display path is wrong and you have spent ₹2L instead of ₹40L finding out.

(parallel with P0)

Legal entity and import rails

Boring, slow, and blocking — start it on day one so it finishes while you prototype.

  • Pvt Ltd (or LLP if staying small) — needed to hold contracts, take grants, invoice.
  • IEC (Import Export Code) from DGFT — blocking you legally cannot import the display module without it.
  • GST registration — needed to claim input credit on components, which is real money at these volumes.
  • Current account + a customs broker you trust.
P1
Months 2–5
₹4–8L

Integration prototype

already done The software stack is not a risk on this project. The language pipeline — self-hosted TTS across languages, NLLB translation, Whisper ASR, the forked glasses app — is built and tested. That is genuinely months of de-risked work most hardware projects still have ahead of them.

What remains is electrical and mechanical:

  • Custom PCB — main board plus flex circuits running into the temples.
  • Power: multi-rail PMIC (the display needs three voltages), charging, battery protection.
  • Frame CAD, 3D printed and iterated. Expect ten-plus print revisions before the optics sit right.
  • Budget 3–4 full PCB spins. First-spin-works is not a plan, it is luck.
Gate

8 hours real battery life · under 35 g · someone wears it through a market and nobody comments on it.

P2
Months 5–7
₹3–5L

EVT — Engineering Validation

5–10 hand-assembled units. Every function works on every unit. This is the first time you build more than one of something, and it is where you discover which assembly steps are impossible with human hands.

  • Write the assembly procedure as you build. It becomes the factory's instructions later.
  • Time each step. Assembly labour is a real per-unit cost you have not counted yet.
P3
Months 7–10
₹5–8L

DVT — Design Validation

20–30 units, built to the real design. This phase exists to break things on purpose.

  • Optical alignment survival — the highest-risk test on this product. Fold the temples 500 times, then check whether the waveguide image is still aligned. A sealed cartridge is precision-bonded internally, but how you mount it in a flexing plastic frame is your problem, not the supplier's.
  • Drop test, sweat and humidity (this is India — assume monsoon and 40 °C), thermal soak in direct sun.
  • Unit-to-unit consistency: does the display sit in the same spot in someone's vision on all 30?
  • Certification samples are pulled from this build.
Gate

30/30 units survive the fold cycle with alignment intact. If not, redesign the optical mount now — after tooling it costs ten times more.

Certification — the hard legal gate

You cannot legally sell, stock, or import a single unit without these. Start them the day DVT hardware exists.

Manufacture in India if you possibly can. BIS CRS for a domestic manufacturer takes 3–5 weeks; for a foreign manufacturer it takes 4–6 months and requires appointing an Authorised Indian Representative. That single choice can cost you half a year.

Crossover is somewhere around 1,500–2,500 units. Below that, printing frames is simply cheaper. Tool up when you have demand proven, not before.

What actually kills this

1 · The display isn't readable enough outdoors
Kills: the entire product thesis. Mitigation: P0 answers it for ₹2L in week four. This is why P0 is first and alone.
2 · The supplier won't sell you 100 units
Optical engine makers are geared to OEMs ordering tens of thousands. Mitigation: ask about MOQ in your very first email, before designing anything around a part you cannot buy. Have a second supplier identified.
3 · Optical alignment drifts in a flexing frame
The cartridge is precision-bonded inside; how you hold it in plastic that bends every time someone folds the temples is entirely your engineering problem. Mitigation: DVT fold-cycle testing, and a metal or composite sub-frame carrying the optics rather than mounting to plastic directly.
4 · Money runs out between DVT and PVT
The classic hardware death. You have spent ~₹20L, have 30 beautiful working units, and need ₹30L more to make 100 that you can legally sell. Mitigation: raise or pre-sell for the whole path to batch one, not to prototype. Use the 30 DVT units as the demo that raises it.
5 · BIS timeline slips into your launch
Mitigation: start certification the day DVT units exist, run it fully parallel, and manufacture domestically so you are on the 3–5 week track instead of the 4–6 month one.

Funding a first batch that ends up affordable

₹52–78L for 100 units cannot come from selling 100 units at ₹18,000. It has to come from somewhere that is not the end user — which is the same conclusion the pricing work reached, arrived at from the other direction.

SourceWhy it fits this specific project
Government pilot orderSell batch one to a state education or agriculture department as a funded pilot. They have bought free laptops and tablets for students before; this is a known procurement shape. Solves funding and distribution to exactly the users this is for.
CSR (Companies Act §135)Large Indian companies are legally required to spend 2% of net profit on CSR, and assistive/education technology qualifies. This is an existing budget line, not a hypothetical grant.
Grant fundingAssistive-technology and accessibility grants exist specifically for this. The language-access angle is genuinely strong here, not a pitch decoration.
Pre-orders at the honest priceUrban and institutional buyers pay ~₹30,000; that margin cross-subsidises units for farm workers and students. Requires the DVT units as proof first.
